Sales grew, but this stock still crashed sixteen percent. You're reading results wrong.
Last Friday, Intellect Design, a banking software company, fell sixteen percent to a fifty two week low. The same day, Laurus Labs, a medicine maker, jumped seven percent to a record high.
Both had just reported September quarter results. Both grew their sales. So why did the market cheer one, and dump the other?
Most people think sales up means stock up. That's the myth. But wait till you see the profit line.
Laurus. Revenue up thirty three percent, to two thousand two hundred crore. Profit up one hundred twenty six percent, to four hundred forty one crore. Margins jumped from twenty six to thirty four percent.
Intellect. Total income up eleven percent. But profit barely moved, one hundred four crore, versus one hundred two crore last quarter. Revenue came in below expectations. The stock is now fifty nine percent below its high.
But a record high has a risk too. Laurus is up about two and a half times in a year, and Kotak still has a sell rating on it. When expectations are this high, even good results can disappoint.
So here's the rule. Stocks don't move on good or bad news. They move on surprise. Check three things. Profit growth, margins, and what the market already expected.
